**Swedish men have lung cancer rates less than HALF the EU average - here’s how harm reduction made the difference.**

When it comes to tobacco harm reduction, Sweden stands out as a global leader with measurable population health benefits. While lung cancer remains a devastating disease across much of Europe, Swedish men enjoy exceptionally low rates - and the evidence shows this success stems from Sweden’s unique, decades-long approach to tobacco harm reduction centered around snus.

## The Striking Lung Cancer Difference

The numbers are impossible to ignore: Swedish male lung cancer incidence is just **39 per 100,000**, compared to the EU average of **95 per 100,000**. That’s less than half the rate seen across the rest of Europe - a remarkable public health achievement that translates to thousands of lives saved each year.

## Sweden’s Remarkable Smoking Decline

This outcome didn’t happen by accident. It’s the result of sustained efforts dating back decades:

- In the early 1980s, **over 30 percent of Swedes smoked daily**
- By 2003, daily smoking rates had fallen to **16 percent** \[GSTHR 2026\]
- Today, **less than 5% of Swedish adults smoke daily** (4.8% according to the 2025 annual report) - while more than **20% of adults in the EU still smoke daily**

{{< figure src="slide3.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

As reported by The Local.se: “For the first time in CAN’s surveys, fewer than five percent smoke regularly in Sweden… the political goal for Sweden to be smoke-free by 2025 has been achieved.” The proportion of daily smokers dropped from 16 to 4.8 percent between 2003 and 2025.

{{< figure src="slide4.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

## The Snus Factor: A Harm Reduction Cornerstone

While preventative measures drove initial declines, Sweden’s approach uniquely combines these efforts with continued availability of snus - the moist tobacco powder placed under the lip that has been a Swedish tradition for centuries.

Sweden remains the benchmark for population-level THR observation because it combines long-term high uptake of snus with very low daily smoking.

{{< figure src="slide5.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

## Quantifying the Benefit: Lives Saved

The impact isn’t just theoretical - it translates to real lives saved. A study by Ramström (2024) directly quantified the mortality benefit of snus-driven smoking displacement in Sweden.

By comparing observed Swedish mortality data with counterfactual prevalence across the EU, the study estimated that **approximately 3,000 lives are saved annually** in Sweden due to snus substituting for cigarettes.

## Understanding the Latency Effect

One important nuance explains why the full cancer benefits aren’t always immediately apparent in all age groups: the latency effect.

Despite per-capita cigarette consumption falling from approximately 1,800 to 600 per year, lung cancer rates respond to combustion among men aged 75–79 in 2021 remained comparable to 1970 levels.

This delay occurs because lung cancer develops slowly over decades of exposure. The historical shift to filter cigarettes and the long latency period mean that today’s lung cancer rates in older cohorts still reflect smoking patterns from years ago.

But encouraging trends already appear in other countries. A statistically significant downward trend in acute coronary disease hospitalisations has been observed in Japan among younger adults aged 20-49, and steeper declines in areas with higher heated tobacco product uptake. Similar patterns are emerging in Sweden for cardiovascular and respiratory health that respond more quickly to reduced smoke exposure.

As younger cohorts who benefited from reduced smoking exposure age into higher-risk categories, we’re beginning to see the full impact emerge across more health indicators.

## The Takeaway: Proof That Long-Term THR Works

Sweden’s experience offers powerful evidence that tobacco harm reduction, when implemented thoughtfully and consistently over decades, can produce measurable population health benefits.

The Swedish model demonstrates that: - Strong preventative measures (tax increases, advertising bans, cessation support) are essential for reducing smoking initiation and increasing cessation - Continued availability of less harmful alternatives like snus provides a viable pathway for smokers unwilling or unable to quit nicotine entirely - A pragmatic approach that recognizes nicotine itself isn’t the primary problem - it’s the smoke - yields tangible public health results over time.

{{< figure src="slide11.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

Such population health improvements are compatible with the reduced combustion exposure experienced in Sweden due to the smoking decline - but when combined with Sweden’s historically low smoking rates, sustained snus use, and quantified lives saved, the case for tobacco harm reduction’s effectiveness becomes compelling.

For countries seeking to reduce smoking-related disease, Sweden’s long-term experiment provides a key lesson: when smokers have access to satisfying, less harmful alternatives, and when those alternatives are regulated sensibly rather than prohibited, significant public health improvements can and do follow over generations.
