**Why Cigarettes Are Everywhere But Non-Combustible Nicotine Alternatives Are Banned?**

Here’s something that should make you stop and think: In 2026, you can walk into almost any store worldwide and buy a pack of cigarettes - a product that kills up to half of its long-term users. But try to buy a vape or nicotine pouch to help quit smoking, and you might be breaking the law.

{{< figure src="slide2.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

This isn’t just an occasional inconsistency - it’s a global pattern that defies logic and costs lives.

Let’s look at the numbers that reveal this shocking paradox:

**First, the legal availability of safer alternatives:**

- Safer nicotine products like vapes, heated tobacco, snus, and nicotine pouches are legally available in **128 countries**

- That covers **3.9 billion people** - about **67% of the global population**

**Now, where these products are completely banned:**

- **73 countries** maintain total prohibitions on ALL safer nicotine products

- That’s **1.9 billion people** - approximately **33% of the world’s population**

- In these places, if you smoke, you have no legal access to less harmful alternatives

And here’s the mind-bending part: **Combustible cigarettes remain legally available EVERYWHERE** - even in those 73 countries that ban all safer alternatives.

{{< figure src="slide4.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

Consider these real-world cases:

- **Bangladesh** criminalized production, import, sale, AND personal use of ALL safer nicotine products - with up to 6 months imprisonment.

- **Mexico** became the first country to embed a vape ban in its constitution, with 1-8 year prison sentences for vape-related activities.

- **France** implemented a ban on non-medicinal oral nicotine products in April 2026, threatening with imprisonment and thousands of euros fines for possession and use of these products.

- **Canada** restricted nicotine pouch sales to pharmacies-only since August 2024, effectively removing them from general availability.

- Across **six ASEAN nations** (Thailand, Singapore, Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam) vapes are banned, with the Philippines likely to follow.

{{< figure src="slide6.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

{{< figure src="slide7.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

Why does this matter? Because when safer alternatives are banned or heavily restricted, smokers who want to reduce their risk have only two options: continue smoking deadly cigarettes or turn to unregulated black markets.

This asymmetry continues to define the central paradox in global tobacco control policy: the most harmful product is universally legal, less harmful alternatives are not.

This isn’t just theoretical. When countries ban vapes while keeping cigarettes available:

- Adult smokers lose access to effective quitting tools

- Some revert to smoking exclusively

- Others seek unregulated products with unknown safety profiles

- Public health opportunities to reduce smoking-related disease are missed

{{< figure src="slide8.en.jpg" class="w-[400px]" >}}

The good news? Change IS happening. Countries like Sweden, New Zealand, and Japan show us what’s possible when regulation follows evidence rather than fear.

**Swedish men**, for instance, have lung cancer rates less than HALF the EU average - largely due to decades of snus use as a cigarette alternative.

The solution isn’t complicated: **regulate safer nicotine products proportionate to their risk** - not ban them while leaving the most deadly option freely available.

Smokers deserve access to less harmful alternatives..
