**Heated Tobacco Products (HTPs)** are electronically controlled devices that heat a tobacco rod in order to produce visible aerosol. Their commercial presence expanded rapidly since launching in Italy and Japan in 2014, and in Greece in 2016. The integration of advanced electronic technology determined the emergence of these products.

## The Failure of Traditional Modifications and the Role of Combustion

The development of HTPs was driven by the historical failure of traditional cigarette modifications to reduce smoking-related health risks. Innovations such as the introduction of cigarette filters, “light” cigarettes, and the use of absorbent substances like active charcoal in filters did not succeed in lowering the actual harm associated with smoking. The only viable way to create a significantly less harmful tobacco product was to entirely bypass combustion (burning), the primary chemical process that generates the vast majority of toxic and carcinogenic chemicals found in traditional cigarette smoke.

## Operating Mechanisms and Temperatures

The core operating principle of HTPs is the electronic control of temperature to prevent tobacco from burning. In a traditional cigarette, the burning tip reaches temperatures as high as 800°C to 850°C. In contrast, HTPs heat tobacco to a range of 250°C to 350°C. This precise heating is controlled electronically through two main mechanisms depending on the device: a metallic heating blade inserted into the tobacco plug for direct heat transmission, or electromagnetic induction heating. Although combustion is bypassed, a certain degree of pyrolysis (thermal decomposition) still occurs, which is visible in the physical changes of the tobacco plug after use

## Scientific and Clinical Evidence

Independent research has provided evidence supporting the harm reduction potential of HTPs compared to combustible cigarettes, mainly by showing significantly reduced toxin exposure. For example toxic aldehyde emissions from HTPs were 85% to 95% lower than those of conventional cigarettes. Reduced cellular adverse effects compared to traditional cigarette smoke have also been observed. Clinical studies examining biomarkers of exposure in human smokers have shown a reduction of up to 90% in exposure to toxic substances. Combustion byproducts, including carbon monoxide and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, are almost entirely absent in HTP aerosol.

## Public Health Implications and Limitations

While HTPs are not completely safe and still expose users to some toxic substances, the dramatic chemical difference justifies their classification as Tobacco Harm Reduction products. Currently, the primary scientific limitation is the lack of long-term epidemiological data in humans to fully quantify their long-term health impact.
