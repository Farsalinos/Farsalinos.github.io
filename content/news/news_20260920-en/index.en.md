*A weekly look at smoking and tobacco harm reduction news — vaping, nicotine pouches, heated tobacco, snus, regulation and science — covering September 14 through September 20, 2026.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The big picture

This was a week defined by a widening split between *science* and *regulation*. New evidence kept reinforcing the case that non-combustible nicotine products help smokers quit, while governments across Asia, Europe and the US moved in different — sometimes opposite — directions on how (or whether) to give adults access to those products.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## United States: Signs of a policy shift at the FDA

The biggest storyline in US harm reduction circles this week was renewed debate over whether the FDA is finally softening its historically adversarial stance toward vaping and other reduced-risk nicotine products.

**Bret Koplow's permanent appointment as CTP director.** Reporting on September 16 highlighted the confirmation of Bret Koplow, Ph.D., J.D., as permanent director of the FDA's Center for Tobacco Products (CTP), after months as acting director. His mandate from HHS explicitly pairs two goals: promoting innovation and access to less-harmful alternatives for adult smokers, while protecting young people from nicotine and tobacco use. Observers pointed to a pattern from his time as acting director — including the FDA's June authorization of a modified-risk claim for 20 ZYN nicotine pouch products (stating that switching completely from cigarettes to ZYN lowers the risk of several serious diseases), an accelerated pilot-program authorization of six more pouch products, and May's first-ever authorization of fruit-flavored e-cigarettes for adult smokers — as evidence of a more risk-proportionate regulatory approach, rather than a wholesale change in enforcement posture. Enforcement against unauthorized products, especially those resembling candy or toys, continues in parallel.

- Source: *Vaping Post*, "After a Decade-Long 'War on Vaping,' Is the FDA Finally Embracing Tobacco Harm Reduction?" (Sept 16, 2026)

**Vapers quitting vapes by taking up cigarettes.** A widely discussed Bloomberg feature (Sept 18) documented an unusual trend: some longtime vapers, frustrated at how hard nicotine vapes are to quit, are deliberately switching to combustible cigarettes as a "harder to use, easier to quit" substitute — reasoning that the friction of smoking (going outside, the smell, needing a lighter) makes it easier to eventually stop altogether than the constant, frictionless availability of a vape. Public health specialists have noted this runs counter to the core premise of tobacco harm reduction, since it trades a lower-risk product for a far more dangerous one. It is indeed a horrible outcome, a direct result of gross misinformation and failed risk communication from the public health community.

- Source: *Bloomberg*, "People Hooked on Vapes Try a New Way to Quit: Cigarettes" (Sept 18, 2026)

## Nicotine pouches: continued growth, continued scrutiny

Nicotine pouches remained one of the fastest-moving categories in the harm reduction space, with the FDA's ongoing authorization pipeline and separate controversy over their use in institutional settings continuing to generate coverage this week, alongside broader industry data on the category's rapid growth in the US market (smokeless tobacco/pouch convenience-store sales are reported to be up sharply year-over-year, driven almost entirely by oral nicotine pouches). New York State's 75% wholesale tax on tobacco-free nicotine pouch products, which took effect September 1, also continued to shape distributor and retailer coverage through the month as the market adjusted to the added cost.

## Heated tobacco: a new device platform launches

**New heated tobacco product in South Korea."** On September 15, a South Korean tobacco company launched its new flagship heated-tobacco device platform, marking a shift from the "circumferential" (external resistance) heating used in earlier heat-not-burn devices to internal induction and microwave heating technology. Other features such as an OLED touchscreen and smartphone connectivity were added. The launch reflects intensifying competition in the heated-tobacco category, particularly in Asian markets.

- Source: *Tobacco Insider* product tracking, week of Sept 15, 2026

## Asia-Pacific: a widening "policy paradox"

This was arguably the most consequential region for harm reduction policy this week, with three separate but connected developments.

**CAPHRA launches its Asia-Pacific Declaration (Sept 15).** The Coalition of Asia Pacific Tobacco Harm Reduction Advocates launched a formal declaration calling on health ministers across the WHO South-East Asia and Western Pacific regions to legalize regulated access to safer nicotine products for adult smokers. The declaration argues that combustible cigarettes — not vapes, pouches or heated tobacco — remain the region's primary deadly nicotine product, and that risk-proportionate regulation, not blanket prohibition, is the more coherent public-health response. It will be formally presented at the Asia Forum on Nicotine in Manila on October 16–17.

- Source: *GlobeNewswire* (Sept 14–15, 2026)

**Malaysia's "accidental" vape ban.** After the Malaysian government withdrew its appeal of a High Court ruling, liquid nicotine reverted to the country's Poisons List — directly conflicting with the 2024 Control of Smoking Products for Public Health Act, which was written specifically to regulate e-cigarettes as consumer products. The clash has left the legal status of ordinary commercial vape sales in question, prompted MPs to call for refunds of roughly RM354 million in previously collected vape excise, and raised concerns among industry groups (representing more than 55,000 vape-sector workers) that insurers and banks may pull back from businesses handling nicotine products. Harm reduction researchers warn the confusion could push consumers toward the illicit market. You can find my article dedicated to this story [here](https://farsalinos.github.io/en/science-deep-dives/malaysia-poison-en/).

**Taiwan's escalating vape prohibition.** Taiwan, which has banned vapes since 2023 while continuing to permit cigarette sales and some heated tobacco products, is now considering fines of up to NT\$100,000 for possession of prohibited e-cigarettes. The move comes amid alarm over illicit "zombie vapes" adulterated with the anesthetic etomidate. Harm reduction researcher Dr. Konstantinos Farsalinos has characterized Taiwan's approach as "risk-inverted" regulation — supporting enforcement against dangerous adulterated products while arguing that the absence of any legal, regulated vape market is precisely what has left consumers dependent on unregulated, unsafe suppliers in the first place. You can find my article dedicated to this story [here](https://farsalinos.github.io/en/science-deep-dives/taiwan-cigarettes-legal-vapes-banned-en/).

- Source: *Vaping Post*, "Asia's Nicotine Policy Paradox: Cigarettes Remain Available While Safer Alternatives Face More Restrictions" (Sept 18, 2026)

The same article notes that Kuwait's incoming 2027 regulations will retain legal pathways for regulated vaping and heated tobacco while imposing much tighter rules on non-medicinal nicotine pouches — another example of inconsistent risk-based treatment across products within a single jurisdiction.

## Europe: divided, but a "development" gives cautious hope

Coverage flagged that Europe's nicotine policy debate is entering what one outlet called a "potentially decisive phase," with competing proposals on flavors, vaping, nicotine pouches and taxation still unresolved across the EU. Within the window, UK-focused coverage continued to track the run-up to two major domestic changes: the new Vaping Products Duty (£2.20 excise per 10ml of e-liquid, plus VAT) taking effect October 1, 2026, and a parallel consultation on plain packaging, flavor-name restrictions and an end to open vape displays in shops, opened in July under the Tobacco and Vapes Act. Industry groups such as UKVIA have continued to campaign against the cumulative weight of new restrictions arriving in close succession.

## Science: two studies worth knowing about

**Second-hand smoke: 1.66 million deaths in 2023.** Published September 7 in *The Lancet Public Health* as part of the Global Burden of Disease study, this large modelling analysis (from more than 1,000 researchers, funded by Bloomberg Philanthropies and the Gates Foundation) estimated that second-hand smoke exposure caused 1.66 million deaths worldwide in 2023 (95% uncertainty interval: 1.33–2.07 million), based on an estimated 2.71 billion people exposed globally, including 767 million children. Coverage this week (Sept 18) walked through the study's methodology and limitations — notably that it used an "all-or-nothing" exposure classification (not accounting for intensity of exposure), excluded public places like bars and restaurants, and applied 2023 exposure levels to 2023 mortality despite the fact that some associated diseases (like lung cancer) take decades to develop after exposure.

- Source: GBD 2023 Second-hand Smoke Collaborators, *The Lancet Public Health* (2026); analysis via *Vaping Post* (Sept 18, 2026)
