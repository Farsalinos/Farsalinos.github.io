**Bottom line (11–18 October 2026):** The week’s biggest developments were regulatory. The European Commission published the results of its Tobacco Products Directive consultation (41,693 submissions), Germany’s Bundestag passed a tobacco tax increase running to 2030, and US states kept tightening vape product directories, with a federal judge upholding Pennsylvania’s law and Iowa delaying its own. New scientific evidence on smoking cessation was thin this week.

## EU: Tobacco Products Directive revision

- **Consultation results published (12 Oct).** The European Commission released its factual summary of the public consultation on revising the Tobacco Products and Tobacco Advertising Directives (open 22 May–14 August 2026). It covers 41,693 submissions. Industry-side and supporting respondents emphasised risk-proportionate rules, adult access to less harmful products and illicit-trade concerns. NGOs and public authorities called for a broad revision aligned with the WHO FCTC, including synthetic-nicotine and disposable products.

## United States: FDA, states and courts

- **FDA names new advisory committee members (14 Oct).** FDA announced four new voting members of the Tobacco Products Scientific Advisory Committee: Holger Behrsing, Kim “Skip” Murray, Margaret Nolan and Andrew A. Strasser. It is also seeking two more. Murray serves as the general-public representative. Advisory membership does not change authorisation policy.

- **Pennsylvania vape directory law upheld (15 Oct).** A federal judge rejected vape companies’ challenge to Act 57, which limits e-cigarette sales to products with FDA authorisation or qualifying pending applications, finding it fits an exception to federal preemption. Enforcement is scheduled to begin 19 October, with fines starting at \$500 per violating product.

- **Iowa postpones its vape directory (15 Oct).** The law in question (House File 2677, signed in May 2024) allows only vape products listed in a public directory kept by the Iowa Department of Revenue to be sold in the state. To be listed, a product must have FDA marketing authorisation or qualify under certain pending federal applications (originally those filed by September 2020). Manufacturers pay \$100 per registered product each year; retailers selling unlisted products face fines of \$300 per product per day and possible permit revocation. On 15 October, the day the restrictions were due to start, the Department said it needs more time to decide which products qualify. It also postponed the 16 November enforcement date, and no new date has been set. Retailers face no new sales restrictions until the directory is published. Vape businesses had sued; a federal judge blocked the law temporarily, and the Eighth Circuit overturned that injunction in July 2026. Iowa is one of at least 16 states with similar directory laws. A study cited by Axios found that Louisiana’s comparable law cut the number of available vape products by nearly 54%, though a decline in sales eventually reversed.

- **New Mexico weighs tougher rules (13 Oct hearing).** At a legislative hearing, lawmakers discussed raising Unfair Practices Act penalties from \$5,000 to \$50,000 per violation, plus flavour limits and broader nicotine taxes. The state’s 25% pouch tax currently applies to tobacco-derived but not synthetic nicotine. Proposals may be considered in the 2027 session.

## Taxation and illicit trade

- **Germany passes tobacco tax rise (15 Oct).** The Bundestag approved revised legislation raising taxes on cigarettes, heated tobacco, rolling tobacco, pipe and hookah tobacco and substitutes through 2030. Cigar and cigarillo increases were cut back and take effect 1 March 2027. The Finance Committee estimated about €1.5 billion in extra revenue in 2027.

- **Italy raises prices on 27 products (effective 14 Oct).** Following updated price lists from the Customs and Monopolies Agency (ADM), prices rose by €0.20 on 13 tobacco products, mainly rolling tobacco and two cigar varieties, and on 14 noncombustible inhalation products. These are price-list increases, which the reports link to Italy’s three-year excise schedule rather than a new excise rate. Cigarettes were excluded because their prices rose earlier in 2026. Under the 2026 budget, the fixed excise component on cigarettes rises from €32 per kg in 2026 to €35.50 in 2027 and €38.50 in 2028; the government projects average cigarette price rises of about €0.25 per pack in 2027 and €0.40 in 2028, depending on brands.

- **Enforcement actions.** German customs seized 115,800 untaxed e-cigarettes (13 Oct); UK authorities in Sunderland seized 188,640 cigarette packs and 74.75 kg of hand-rolling tobacco, valued above £1 million (14 Oct); China reported destroying \$355 million of illicit tobacco and nicotine products (12 Oct).

## Australia

- **Illicit tobacco reform push (13 Oct).** In a parliamentary speech, Julian Hill outlined proposed reforms on illicit-tobacco offences, supply-chain obligations and recovery of criminal profits, citing higher legal cigarette sales in Queensland and spikes in Quitline calls during enforcement. Neither figure alone shows sustained quitting.

## Evidence and research

- **Dependence among young adult vapers (UCLA report, 15 Oct).** Using the 2025 California Health Interview Survey, the report found that 82% of adults aged 18–29 who vaped in the past month had experienced at least one of eight dependence signs, and 64% reported at least one barrier to quitting. Self-reported survey data; it does not show outcomes by former-smoker status.

- **Reporting guidance (14 Oct).** The Association of Health Care Journalists advised reporters to cover both vaping’s cessation potential and its risks, and to separate established smokers from young people who never smoked.
